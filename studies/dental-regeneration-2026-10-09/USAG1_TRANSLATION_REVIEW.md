# USAG-1 tooth-germ activation: animal mechanism and clinical-stage boundary

**Source check: 2026-10-10.** This review tracks the anti-USAG-1 approach as a
developmental tooth-activation strategy. It does not provide a treatment
recommendation or a clinical efficacy assessment.

## What the evidence supports

The 2021 primary study reports that USAG-1 loss or selected neutralizing
antibodies rescued tooth agenesis in several mouse genetic models. In mice,
some antibodies also induced extra or fused teeth; antibodies #12, #16 and #48
were associated with low birth and survival rates in the tested maternal
administration setting. Antibody #57 induced supernumerary and fused teeth in
wild-type mice and showed faint cross-reactivity with SOST. In postnatal ferrets,
antibody #37 produced an additional maxillary incisor described as third-
dentition-like. The ferret result required a fivefold higher concentration,
three administrations and immunosuppression; the reported root appeared short.
This is evidence for restarting tooth development in animal models, not for a
normally rooted, load-bearing replacement tooth in people. See the [primary
Science Advances article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7880588/).

The published antibody series (#37, #57 and others) should remain distinct from
the later clinical candidate, humanized antibody TRG035. The sponsor reports
preclinical efficacy in mouse and beagle models and selection of TRG035, but
those sponsor summaries are not the same evidence record as the peer-reviewed
2021 experiments. The [2024 hospital announcement](https://www.kitano-hp.or.jp/english/info/20240503)
describes the planned first-in-human study and the sponsor's mouse/beagle
preclinical claims.

## Human development status

The Phase I registry record describes a single-dose intravenous,
double-blind, placebo-controlled dose-escalation study with a target of 30
adult men. Its primary outcome is safety; secondary outcomes are
pharmacokinetics and serum anti-TRG035 antibodies. It does not specify tooth
formation as an efficacy endpoint. The official [latest jRCT record](https://jrct.mhlw.go.jp/en-latest-detail/jRCT2051240154)
lists recruitment as “Complete,” with the record last modified October 3,
2025, and no results posted. A prior retrieval through the legacy
`/pages/en-detail/87909/jRCT/3` route returned “Pending”; the canonical latest
record resolves that discrepancy. The sponsor also reported Phase I completion
in its [November 2025 disclosure](https://toregem.co.jp/en/archives/8456).
Recruitment completion and sponsor-reported safety do not establish tooth
regeneration or clinical efficacy.

The August 17, 2026 [Phase IIa trial notice](https://toregem.co.jp/en/english2025/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Toregem_Compleatuion-of-the-PMDA-Investigation-of-CTN-TRG035-P2-.pdf)
reports that PMDA's required review of the clinical-trial notification was
complete. It says enrollment and dosing are planned after review by the trial
site's IRB. The proposed study is an uncontrolled, open-label, multicenter
dose-exploration study in children with severe congenital partial anodontia,
with a target of 24 participants. This was the latest trial-specific sponsor
status located in official company updates through October 10, 2026; the actual
enrollment status after August is therefore unknown. A completed notification
review is a regulatory milestone, not evidence of enrollment, dosing or tooth
formation.

The Phase I population and planned Phase IIa population target congenital
missing-tooth conditions. They do not establish efficacy for adults whose
tooth was lost after extraction, decay, trauma or periodontal disease. The
mechanism depends on developmental tooth tissue being available to respond;
whether a viable arrested germ exists must be treated as a population-specific
question, not assumed for an acquired-loss site.

## Tooth-germ imaging and the organoid connection

A 2026 [Journal of Oral Biosciences imaging study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.job.2026.100775)
describes MRI combined with CT/X-ray markers to visualize dental lamina and
pre-calcified tooth germs in ferret pups aged 0–25 days. This may inform how
congenital cases are staged or selected. The abstract reports an animal imaging
model; it does not establish a validated human surrogate endpoint or prove that
TRG035 regenerates teeth. The article's indexed affiliations include Toregem
BioPharma, so future clinical imaging claims should be checked against
pre-specified validation data.

This developmental activation route complements, but does not replace, the
human iPSC ameloblast-organoid work. The 2026 organoid paper demonstrates an
enamel-forming epithelial component in an ectopic graft; USAG-1 development
work aims to activate tooth germs. Neither source supplies an adult-human
whole-tooth result with a coordinated crown-root structure, vascularized and
sensitive pulp, cementum–PDL–bone attachment, gingival seal, occlusion and
durability. The component evidence remains in the [ameloblast review](AMELOBLAST_ORGANOID_REVIEW.md)
and the [whole-tooth component roadmap](COMPONENT_GAPS.md).

## What would close the translational gap

The near-term decisive evidence is a reported clinical dataset with a declared
tooth-germ eligibility method, participant-level denominator, tooth-level
outcomes and follow-up. Development-stage imaging should be linked to tooth
identity, position, eruption, root formation and unwanted extra/fused teeth.
For claims of functional restoration, tooth-site outcomes must also include
root attachment, periodontal mobility/load response, pulp vitality or sensory
function, occlusion and durability. Adults with acquired loss require a
separately justified mechanism and evidence; success in congenital agenesis
cannot be carried over automatically.

These endpoints are proposed evidence questions, not a clinical protocol. The
