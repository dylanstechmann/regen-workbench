# Periodontal ligament regeneration: model-by-model evidence comparison

This is a focused comparison of four frequently conflated PDL studies. It is not a meta-analysis: the studies use different species, cell sources, treatment sites, denominators and endpoints, so their sample counts and effects should not be pooled.

| Study | Model and cell source | Denominator and follow-up | What was measured/reported | Main evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Dangaria et al., 2011](https://doi.org/10.1089/scd.2010.0431) | Mouse PDL progenitors preseeded onto denuded rat molar roots, then replanted into athymic rat sockets | 4 rats; 8 molars total (4 preseeded, 4 cell-free). The article conflicts on 6 weeks versus 6 months. | Histology described oriented PDL fibers; the authors report all four preseeded teeth retained occlusion without observed root resorption. Among controls, two teeth were lost and two showed resorption/ankylosis. | Cross-species replantation of natural teeth, not iPSC production or chronic periodontitis treatment. Teeth are nested within four rats. The occlusion observation is not a direct tooth-mobility, bite-force or load-transfer measurement. |
| [Chien et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-18415-6) | Rat-fibroblast iPSCs plus BMP-6 in hydrogel, placed in a rat maxillary molar root-surface defect | 16 rats across 4 arms (n=4/arm); 42 days | The combined iPSC+BMP-6 arm had the strongest reported micro-CT bone metrics and histologic new cementum/PDL. Ankylosis was reported in both BMP-6 arms. | Orthotopic defect evidence, but small and short. No iPSC-only arm, direct mechanical function or long-term safety endpoint. Mineral gain can coexist with loss of the ligament space. |
| [Yamashita et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1089/scd.2024.0122) | Feeder-free iPSC-derived PDL stem-cell-like cells with beta-TCP scaffold in a subcutaneous immunodeficient-mouse graft | Eight weeks; the indexed abstract reviewed here does not give graft/animal counts or identify the iPSC line. | Abstract reports osteocalcin-positive bone/cementum-like tissue and collagen-I-positive PDL-like fibers. | Ectopic graft histology does not show insertion at both root cementum and alveolar bone, periodontal-space organization, mobility or load transfer. Denominator and line identity remain unknown in the abstract. |
| [Li et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.4012/dmj.2025-235) | Human iPSC-derived PDL-like cells differentiated using conditioned media from three PDL preparations | One iPSC line (HPS0381), three differentiation batches; assays typically n=3 | In-vitro morphology, proliferation, PDL-associated markers, osteogenic induction, matrix response and gene-expression response to compression. Results varied by batch; one batch had weaker marker results. | Human cell-source characterization only: no tooth-site graft, two-interface fiber insertion, physiological mobility, load transfer or long-term in-vivo stability. Batches do not substitute for independent donor lines. |

## What the comparison supports

The strongest anatomical evidence among these four is still from small rat experiments using natural replanted teeth or a surgically created root defect. Those studies answer different questions: root-surface preseeding suggests that a natural root can guide progenitor organization, while the BMP-6 study shows that a strong mineralizing signal can also coincide with ankylosis. Neither demonstrates a mechanically tested, durable ligament regenerate.

The human iPSC work occupies two earlier evidence stages. Li et al. characterize selected in-vitro behaviors from one line with batch variation. Yamashita et al. add an ectopic graft histology signal, but the abstract does not expose the animal/graft denominator or line identity. These studies do not yet bridge to an orthotopic human-cell construct with demonstrated functional attachment.

The comparison therefore supports a cautious research priority: resolve whether an intervention rebuilds a continuous, oriented cementum-PDL-bone interface while preserving a ligament space and tooth mobility. Bone volume, mineralized tissue, PDL markers, collagen fibers and retained occlusion are separate observations; none alone proves load-bearing PDL function. Do not count teeth, defects or culture batches as independent animals or donors.

## Next discriminating evidence

- Report the biological unit and full allocation: donor/iPSC line, clone, independent differentiation batch, animal, tooth, defect and graft, with nesting retained in analysis.
- At the tooth site, quantify insertion at both cementum and alveolar bone, fiber orientation, periodontal-space width, root resorption and ankylosis.
- Measure mobility and mechanical response under a defined physiological loading assessment; histology alone cannot supply this endpoint.
- Replicate iPSC-derived findings across unrelated lines and independent differentiations, with a primary PDL-cell comparator and longer-term genomic-stability/residual-pluripotency follow-up.

These are evidence requirements, not a wet-lab protocol or a claim that a clinically ready treatment exists.
