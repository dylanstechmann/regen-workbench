# Gingival recession: cell adjuncts, coverage and the missing barrier evidence

This focused review compares two human recession studies with different cell
products, scaffolds and designs. It is an endpoint audit, not a pooled estimate,
clinical recommendation or regenerative protocol. Root coverage is not itself
evidence of a restored epithelial seal, native gingival identity, periodontal
ligament insertion or whole-tooth regeneration.

## Studies and observed outcomes

| Study | Design and intervention | Reported signal | Main limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Zanwar et al., 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5463777/) | Small randomized clinical study of multiple recession defects. The test used umbilical-cord-derived cells seeded on a polylactic/polyglycolic-acid membrane; the control used the membrane without the added cells. The paper reports 14 cases and six-month follow-up. | Mean root-coverage percentage was 66.26% versus 57.38% (reported p=.001). Absolute recession-depth reduction was 1.57±0.89 versus 1.24±0.47 mm (p=.35); clinical-attachment gain was 1.78±1.1 versus 1.74±0.62 mm (p=.92); complete root coverage was 45.23% versus 21.42% (p=.36). | The paper's abstract/methods report 16 test and 14 control sites at baseline, while its results refer to 18 and 17 defects. Sites are clustered within a small number of cases. The denominator discrepancy and unit of analysis limit interpretation. Six months does not establish durable coverage or barrier function. |
| [Sankar et al., 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13293744/) | Split-mouth randomized trial: 10 men, 20 sites, 10 per side; both sides received a coronally advanced flap (CAF) and volume-stable collagen matrix, with autologous gingival fibroblasts seeded on the test-side matrix. Follow-up was six months. The cells are fibroblasts, not a characterized stem-cell population. | At six months, the paper reports lower residual recession depth (0.1±0.32 vs 0.6±0.52 mm, p=.025), recession width (0 vs 0.8±0.63 mm, p=.011) and clinical attachment level (0.5±0.53 vs 1.5±0.53 mm, p=.008) for test versus control. Plaque and probing depth did not differ significantly. Thick keratinized tissue was present at 10/10 versus 6/10 sites (p=.087); esthetic, root-coverage-esthetic and pain scores were not significantly different. | Only 10 participants were enrolled, all men, with selected Miller Class I/II defects and six-month follow-up. No histology, direct epithelial-seal assay, recurrence endpoint or ligament-insertion endpoint was reported. The study cannot establish durability, rare-event safety or generalizability. |

The 2017 study's favorable mean root-coverage percentage is not matched by a
significant between-group difference in the reported recession-depth reduction,
attachment gain or complete-coverage proportion. Those outcomes should remain
separate rather than being compressed into an overall “regeneration” result.

The 2026 comparison is more directly paired by participant, but it is a small,
short-term comparison of fibroblast-seeded versus unseeded matrix on top of the
same flap procedure. The clinical differences are preliminary estimates: the
paper provides no confidence intervals for the between-side contrasts, and its
Methods do not describe a correction across the multiple outcomes and time
points examined.

## Reporting and design checks

- In the 2026 paper, the abstract says clinical-attachment gain was significant
  at each follow-up, while the results table reports a three-month intergroup
  p-value of .053. The table therefore does not support describing that
  particular interval as statistically significant.
- The 2026 narrative says baseline clinical parameters were comparable, but
  Table 1 gives a baseline plaque-index p-value of .046. This discrepancy
  should be resolved before relying on the baseline-comparability claim.
- Its sample-size paragraph describes a minimum number “per group,” although
  the trial is split-mouth with 10 participants and paired sites. The enrolled
  patient count and paired design are the safer unit to retain.
- The 2017 report's defect counts differ between baseline methods/abstract and
  results. The available summary means cannot repair that mismatch or recover
  patient-level clustering.

These issues do not erase the reported observations. They narrow what can be
inferred from them and should be surfaced in any later evidence synthesis.

## What the evidence supports—and what it leaves open

The studies provide short-term human evidence that cell-containing adjuncts
have been evaluated for recession coverage: an umbilical-cord-derived cell and
polymer-membrane combination in 2017, and autologous gingival fibroblasts on a
volume-stable collagen matrix in 2026. The 2026 study reports favorable
between-side clinical measurements at six months in its small selected sample.
Neither study demonstrates a durable epithelial barrier, reduced long-term
recurrence, native-like gingival microanatomy, restored cementum–ligament–bone
attachment, or a regenerated tooth.

The next evidence comparison should retain participant and site denominators,
paired allocation, prespecified endpoints and follow-up windows; report
between-group effect estimates with confidence intervals; and distinguish
coverage from barrier integrity and recurrence. Longer, more diverse studies
and direct tissue characterization would be needed to address those gaps.
