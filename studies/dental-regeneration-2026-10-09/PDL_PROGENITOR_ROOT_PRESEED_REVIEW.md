# PDL progenitor root-preseeding study review

This review covers Dangaria et al. (2011), [“Successful Periodontal Ligament
Regeneration by Periodontal Progenitor Preseeding on Natural Tooth Root
Surfaces”](https://doi.org/10.1089/scd.2010.0431), PMID 21250830, PMCID
PMC3210028. It is a small animal tooth-replantation study, not a human treatment
study or a tooth-generation result.

## What the study reports

The authors compared periodontal-ligament (PDL) progenitors with dental-pulp,
dental-follicle and bone-marrow cells on mineral surfaces. In vitro, the PDL
progenitors formed fibers aligned perpendicular to natural root surfaces;
synthetic apatite did not reproduce that orientation. For the in-vivo arm,
mouse PDL progenitors were seeded onto denuded rat molar roots before
replantation into athymic nude rats.

The reported in-vivo denominator was four rats and eight molars: four
cell-preseeded roots and four cell-free replants. Histology described a new PDL
with polarized, parallel-oriented fibers on the preseeded roots. All four
preseeded teeth reportedly retained physiological occlusion without observed
root resorption. Of the four cell-free controls, two were lost; the remaining
two had resorption and ankylosis.

## Evidence boundary

The paper's follow-up timing is internally inconsistent: its abstract and
Methods state six months, while one Results sentence says histology was
performed after six weeks. The record preserves both statements rather than
choosing one. Teeth are nested within only four animal hosts, so eight molars
do not mean eight independent animals. The report describes physiological
occlusion but does not directly measure tooth mobility, bite force or load
transfer.

This is a cross-species progenitor-cell model using replanted natural teeth,
not an iPSC-derived construct, chronic periodontal-disease repair, or
whole-tooth regeneration. Its signal supports further study of root-surface
guidance and PDL orientation; it does not establish a reproducible or
mechanically validated treatment.

## Next discriminator

A replicated study should resolve the timing and analysis unit prospectively,
retain animal-level allocation and denominators, measure both-end fiber
insertion and root resorption/ankylosis, and test function directly under
controlled physiological loading. These are evidence requirements, not a
laboratory protocol.
