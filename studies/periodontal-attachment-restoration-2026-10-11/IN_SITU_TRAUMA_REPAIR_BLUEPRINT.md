# In-situ tooth-trauma repair: engineering blueprint

## Research decision

The evidence supports a focused engineering program for rebuilding a functional
cementum–periodontal ligament (PDL)–alveolar-bone interface. It does not yet
show that a biologic treatment can restore an in-socket tooth after blunt
luxation, return it to its pre-injury position, and keep it there after a
temporary stabilizer is removed. Most positive studies start with an extracted
tooth, a surgically created periodontal defect, or an ectopic implant site.
That model gap should drive the next experiments.

Separate two outcomes throughout the program:

1. **Position:** the tooth's three-dimensional location and angulation relative
   to an unaffected pretreatment reference.
2. **Attachment:** a living, vascularized, correctly oriented, load-bearing PDL
   inserted into cementum and alveolar bone, without ankylosis or root
   resorption.

Restoring attachment alone cannot be counted as correction of a tooth that has
already settled into a different position. Position is measured against a
pretreatment image or an anatomically registered contralateral reference.
Temporary stabilization can be standardized during model development, but the
durability endpoint is assessed after it is removed.

## What the existing experiments establish

| Strategy | Model and result | What it contributes | What remains unproven |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDL cell sheet | Delayed canine replantation; the study reports favourable histologic healing in 88.4% with multilayer PDL cell sheets versus 5.3% in controls at 8 weeks, with new PDL and cementum. | Cell delivery can improve attachment healing in a severe replantation model. | No retained in-socket luxation model, durable function, or position after fixation withdrawal. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21907971/) |
| Endogenous cell homing | Beagle roots had PDL stripped, were air-dried, decoronated, and replanted; EDTA plus SDF1/BMP7 coating produced complete histologic healing in 5/12 roots at 6 months. The remaining treated roots still showed some ankylosis or resorption. | Local recruitment signals plus root-surface conditioning can produce inserted collagen fibers and vascularized PDL-like tissue without cell expansion. | Three dogs supplied the roots; root-level results are clustered within animals. SDF1, BMP7, EDTA, and release kinetics were not separated; complete healing was below half. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4647325/) |
| Fiber-guiding multiphasic scaffold | A CT-designed scaffold was tested in rat periodontal fenestration defects after cementum removal. Oriented PDL-like fibers and attachments to tooth and bone were more consistent with the guided architecture than with an amorphous scaffold; 9/12 guided constructs retained a PDL architecture at 6 weeks. | Geometry and fiber orientation matter alongside cell identity and mineral formation. | This is a surgically opened defect with short follow-up, not restoration of an intact socket after blunt trauma. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4074763/) |
| Layered BMP carrier | In a monkey furcation-defect study, a double-layer collagen-membrane approach was reported to regenerate cementum, ligament, and alveolar process with perpendicular collagen fibers and no ankylosis at 12 weeks; the single-layer approach often ankylosed. | Spatially separating regenerative compartments may reduce bone bridging across the ligament space. | Older defect-model result; no in-socket trauma, long-term positional test, or current manufacturing specification. [Study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9541226/) |
| Human PDL–bone microtissue | A 3D-bioprinted, two-cell human model maintained shape under microfluidic flow for more than 10 days, with cell viability beyond 7 days and PDL-fibroblast/osteoblast layer cohesion and migration. | A human-cell interface can support early material, perfusion, and dose-screening work. | It does not yet recreate a cementum-facing root, aligned inserting fibers, mature neurovascularized ligament, or tooth-scale mechanical function. [Study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gendis.2020.11.011) |

These are preclinical feasibility signals across different injury models and
endpoints. Their percentages must not be pooled or described as human success
rates.

## Staged engineering program

### Gate 1 — Lock the native restoration contract

Use the existing [restoration contract](../restoration-run-plan-2026-10-11/PLAN.md)
and add four gates before any intervention is ranked:

- **Interface:** continuous cementum-facing and bone-facing attachment, with
  collagen bundles inserted at both mineralized boundaries and a maintained
  ligament space.
- **Mechanics:** force–displacement response, hysteresis/energy dissipation,
  stress relaxation, and recovery after cyclic load compared with matched
  healthy tissue. A stiff response alone can be ankylosis.
- **Safety:** quantify ankylosis, inflammatory and replacement root resorption,
  pulp status, vascularity, inflammation, and ectopic mineralization separately.
- **Position and durability:** track tooth position before injury, during
  repair, and after temporary fixation removal; test for drift under repeated
  physiologic loading over months.

Histology, transcript markers, bone volume, or a single pull-out value cannot
substitute for this combined contract.

### Gate 2 — Build a human-cell interface model

Adapt the published PDL–bone microtissue as a screening chassis, then add the
features missing for tooth support: a mineralized root/cementum-facing surface,
a defined PDL-width compartment, anisotropic fiber guidance, and an alveolar
bone-facing compartment. Keep PDL fibroblasts/PDL progenitors and osteogenic
cells spatially distinct; add perfused endothelial support only after the
two-interface structure is stable. Keep gingival epithelium as a separate
outer-tissue module rather than mixing it into the PDL readout.

First compare architecture without adding complex factor cocktails: aligned
versus random fibers, biphasic versus homogeneous matrix, and perfused versus
static culture. Then compare cell-sheet delivery and endogenous-cell-homing
signals as separate factors. Measure cell identity, fiber orientation and
insertion, mineral deposition location, viability, inflammatory response,
leachables, and low-amplitude cyclic mechanics. Include a bone-bridge/ankylosis
positive-control signature so the assay can reject a construct that is strong
only because it is too stiff.

### Gate 3 — Separate injury models instead of extrapolating between them

Maintain three model tracks with separate conclusions:

1. **Retained-tooth luxation / stretch:** intact tooth and socket; quantify
   displacement and PDL injury while preserving the native root surface. This
   is the model that directly addresses loosened or crooked teeth after impact.
2. **Root-surface injury / delayed replantation:** use as a severe attachment
   loss positive-control model for cell sheets or homing cues, not as a proxy
   for an in-socket tooth.
3. **Periodontal defect:** use to benchmark fiber-guiding and multiphasic
   scaffolds where bone and cementum must be rebuilt around a defect.

Before animal testing, validate measurement repeatability in an ex-vivo
tooth–socket loading setup and the human-cell interface model. For any later
animal study, predefine the biological unit, randomize at the animal level,
blind imaging and histology, account for multiple teeth nested within each
animal, and use a time course long enough to distinguish transient attachment
from stable repair. Preserve an untreated injury arm and an established
healing-care comparator; do not define success by reduced mobility alone.

### Gate 4 — Make the decisive durability test

The decisive test begins only after a candidate passes interface, mechanics,
and safety gates. Record tooth coordinates and socket geometry at baseline,
after injury, and after recovery. Remove temporary stabilization at a
prespecified point, then assess position, physiologic mobility, repeated-load
response, periodontal space, fiber insertion, root integrity, pulp vitality,
and inflammation at short and long follow-up. A repair that needs continuing
external force to hold alignment has not met the requested restoration target.

## Next concrete work items

1. Specify imaging registration and tooth-position error bounds using the
   repository's CT/displacement model; keep measurement error separate from
   biological drift.
2. Add the two-interface human PDL–bone model to the organoid track with a
   materials/geometry matrix and acceptance criteria for fiber insertion.
3. Search for a published, reproducible retained-tooth luxation model with
   longitudinal tooth-position data. If none qualifies, document the model
   gap rather than silently substituting replantation.
4. Pre-register the minimum animal-level design only after the ex-vivo assay
   demonstrates repeatable mechanical and ankylosis discrimination.

No human treatment protocol is implied by this research blueprint. Acute
trauma care and research-grade regenerative reconstruction are separate
questions; this program evaluates models and evidence rather than directing
care.
