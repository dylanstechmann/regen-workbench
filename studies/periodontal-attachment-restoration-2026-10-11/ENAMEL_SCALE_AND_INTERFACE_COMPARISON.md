# Missing enamel: demonstrated depth, substrate and next engineering decision

Reviewed 2026-10-17. This comparison supports the
[full-tooth target](FULL_TOOTH_RESTORATION_BLUEPRINT.md), including original
form, material organization, internal integrity and living attachment. It
does not select an intervention for a person. No materials were made or
applied in this computational project.

## Primary evidence comparison

Reported layer depths below describe particular experiments, not treatment
effect ranges or pooled estimates. A coating deposited before mineralization
and the final mineralized layer must remain distinct measurements.

| Lead | Substrate/model | Demonstrated depth or compartment | Interface/function evidence | Unresolved target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shao 2019, CPIC mineralization | Extracted human enamel | Approximately 2.0-2.8 micrometres; whole-surface example approximately 2.7 | Epitaxial rod/inter-rod continuity; laboratory indentation and friction | Bulk enamel, exposed-dentin transition, living-tooth durability |
| Shao 2019, repeated-growth supplement | Extracted enamel | Figure S10: approximately 5 micrometres after two cycles; 10 after four | Images support thickening; full-depth repeated-load integrity not established here | Scale-up, interlayer defects, crack resistance and residual growth limit |
| Hasan 2025, ELR matrix | Extracted human enamel | Prismatic example approximately 2 micrometres; aprismatic example approximately 10 | Oriented mineral growth, integration, indentation, wear and laboratory challenges | Full-thickness crown geometry and clinical durability |

| Patni 2026, ameloblast organoids | Human iPSC-derived cells; mouse kidney-capsule graft | Enamel-like calcified material; no qualified adult-tooth repair thickness here | Cell maturation and ectopic material formation | Controlled crown thickness, adult-tooth integration and load-bearing durability |

[Hasan primary article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64982-y),

The existing [ameloblast source review](../dental-regeneration-2026-10-09/AMELOBLAST_ORGANOID_REVIEW.md)
contains the organoid qualifications. None of these entries demonstrates the
complete integrated restoration target in a mature human tooth.

The repeated-growth observation is a positive engineering lead. It prevents
dismissing layering as wholly untested. It also does not show that repeating
four cycles indefinitely yields bulk tissue of uniform quality. The images
and caption do not supply a qualified thickness distribution or independent
specimen count for an efficacy estimate. Shao declares no competing interests.

Hasan reports ex-vivo mineralization and notes that in-vivo validation remains
needed. Two authors disclose cofounder/equity interests in Mintech-Bio. Record
that commercial context alongside the measurements; it does not determine
their validity. The article also records disagreement in the literature over
native DEJ epitaxy, so one crystallographic feature is insufficient to define
complete junction equivalence.

## Scale arithmetic, without extrapolated efficacy

For an **illustrative** missing depth of 0.5 mm, a 10-micrometre layer spans
2% of the required depth. For 1 mm, it spans 1%. Neither defect depth is a
measurement from a user or this reference specimen. These ratios are not a
forecast of treatment count, growth rate, time or success.

Missing volume requires a region-specific target and current surface, with
uncertainty; a layer depth does not establish restored volume without area,
continuity and shape information. A target thickness inferred from a photo
must not enter this calculation as an exact historical measurement.

## Engineering priority and falsification

**The closest material branch to test is controlled acellular growth on
surviving enamel, with exposed dentin treated as a separate interface
challenge.** This is a project prioritization inference from demonstrated
host continuity and thin-layer function, not a claim of clinical superiority.
The cell-generated branch remains relevant to tissue manufacture and
replacement where an appropriate mature surface cannot guide growth.

The next useful comparison should measure an intended thickness ladder on
characterized extracted-tooth research specimens. First reproduce the
published thin-layer result, then test a predeclared increase in depth;
challenge each new depth before advancing. Choose the ladder and margins
from specimen geometry, instrumentation resolution and controls, not from
favorable results. No fabrication recipe or patient application protocol is
specified here.

Keep these three substrates separate:

- **Surviving enamel:** measure orientation through depth and continuity with
  the host, including regional prism organization and material composition.
- **Exposed dentin:** additionally measure the reconstructed transition,
  collagen/mineral organization, permeability and fracture behavior across
  the interface. A hard superficial deposit is insufficient.
- **Restorative-material interface:** characterize adhesion or discontinuity
  as its own boundary. Success on native mineral does not establish success
  next to a filling; removal of existing material is not an assumed step.

At each depth retain reconstructed volume and surface error, porous/organic
fraction, interlayer discontinuity, crack initiation/propagation, interface
failure mode and cyclic wet-load performance. Include matched intact,
damaged, established restorative and appropriate process controls. Keep tooth
and donor identifiers; multiple indentations or images from one tooth do not
become independent specimens. Preserve every failure and unassessed domain.

Scaling fails if increased thickness produces misorientation, weak layers,
uncontrolled contours, leakage, crack propagation or incompatibility with
living tissues. A shaped prefabricated enamel-like insert is a separate
manufacturing hypothesis if surface growth cannot span a defect; it would
still need a durable junction and must not inherit claims of native regrowth.

Finally, test the completed crown with pulp and periodontal endpoints: shape
changes contacts and force transfer into the PDL. Preventing mineralization
of the soft attachment space remains an independent requirement. The
[restoration contract](restoration_contract.json) keeps these domain gates
separate.
