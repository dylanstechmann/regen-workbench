# Post-challenge dentin-interface source comparison

This is an exploratory, source-inspected analysis. Two new primary candidates were screened; the intake stopped after the Turkistani 2024 source workbook resolved. No prior Shao, Hasan, HEA or Theisen dataset is reanalyzed. No additional sources, author contacts or image digitization are required.

- Extract every factual numeric observation from the original `OCT data` worksheet: four columns, five ten-row blocks each, separated by blank rows. Preserve the source addresses and every repeated value. Do not generate tooth or donor IDs from row positions.
- Primary descriptive results are the 50 source scan-row values per group: mean, median, range and sample SD. Label the SD as a scan-row statistic, not an independent-tooth or donor SD. Do not calculate SEM, confidence intervals, ANOVA or a new p value from scans.
- The paper reports five teeth per group and ten scans per tooth, averaged. Analyze the five worksheet blocks conditionally as that nesting: mean of each block and sample SD of the five means. Check whether the rounded results reproduce published Table 2. Numerical reconciliation supports the segmentation but cannot recover specimen identity.
- Compute CHX-minus-no-CHX differences of source means separately within etch-and-rinse and self-etch groups, in percentage points. The four treatment columns are unpaired groups. Do not match blocks across columns or describe the difference as an individual treatment response, clinical effect or causal interaction estimate.
- As a record-level sensitivity, delete one ten-row source block independently from each contrasted group. Report the minimum and maximum contrast across all 25 combinations. These are deletion-sensitivity ranges, not confidence intervals, actual tooth-loss bounds or efficacy ranges.
- Audit exact repeated runs of at least three values between blocks. Retain them. A repeated value sequence does not establish duplication, provenance error or independent confirmation; source identity records are needed to distinguish those possibilities.
- Keep the nominal 2 mm cavity depth and 4 mm width separate from actual per-specimen repair geometry, which is missing. OCT is measured after 10,000 thermal cycles; there is no pre-aging OCT baseline or mechanical fatigue history. Do not infer thickness dependence, change during aging, survival probability, cyclic chewing durability or regenerated tooth tissue.
- Preserve the source's beam tensile means and failure-mode counts as unlinked aggregate facts. The workbook has no raw tensile data or shared physical IDs. Do not pool these outcomes with OCT or compute a cross-endpoint correlation. Unreported losses and pretest failures remain unknown.
- The measured endpoint is selected cavity-floor width with enhanced binary OCT signal after source silver processing. It is not directly a fluid permeability, gap-depth distribution, total restored volume or complete barrier-function measurement. The paper's reported p=0.068 is not evidence of equivalence.

The next physical question remains whether actual intended-depth restoration on a known host region retains its interface through wet repeated loading. The current source supplies a post-challenge established-restorative reference only. Natural enamel/dentin, pulp, native PDL/cementum/socket, gums, sensation and stable original position after finite support withdrawal remain required outcomes.
